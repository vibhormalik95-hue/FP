\section{Research question}
\begin{frame}{Experiment Two results}
\small
100 fresh synthetic requests with model-generated references.\\
Llama: 76 exact, 23 rejected, one accepted mismatch.\\
Rules: 11/100 exact matches.

Intended-control success combines requested counts\\
with a change in ranked category exposure.\\
Llama: 20.145\%; item feedback: 3.815\%.\\
Conditional requests: no control successes.\\
NDCG@10: 0.02580 Llama versus 0.03184 item feedback.
\vfill
\tiny The earlier exposed practice score of 73/100 is separate. Model-generated references are not human validation.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Research question}
Can one natural-language interaction satisfy a richer intended change than one item reaction?
\vspace{0.35cm}
\begin{itemize}
\item A compound request can specify several categories, strength and exceptions.
\item The LLM must also outperform a rule parser to establish its own contribution.
\item Offline compliance and historical prediction measure different outcomes.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.2cm}
The claim that language \emph{feels more natural} requires human evidence beyond this study.
\end{frame}

\section{Experiment 1}
\begin{frame}{Translation and enforcement are separate}
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{2.2cm}P{7.9cm}@{}}
\toprule
Stage & Role\\\midrule
Recommender & Frozen SASRec-style scores for the candidate catalogue.\\[0.18cm]
Translator & Llama 3.1 8B or rules compile language into a validated command.\\[0.18cm]
Enforcer & Shared deterministic reranking applies the command.\\[0.18cm]
Explanation & Verified facts describe the displayed item and control.\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\vspace{0.35cm}
A correct hard exclusion comes from the enforcement code. It does not demonstrate language understanding.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Data and information boundaries}
\begin{itemize}
\item KuaiRec cohort: \textbf{865 users}, \textbf{2,593 candidates}, 31 opaque category identifiers.
\item Positive event: watch ratio $>2$, an engagement proxy.
\item Global chronological windows: nominal 70\% training, 15\% request history, 15\% held-out evaluation.
\item Requests use training and request history only.
\item Quality target: first eligible held-out positive item.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.2cm}
Synthetic requests and numeric category labels limit claims about real preferences and language.
\src{Filtered cohort counts, not the upstream KuaiRec matrix size. The candidate list could include items users had already seen.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Five matched configurations}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}cP{8.9cm}@{}}
\toprule
ID & Mechanism\\\midrule
A & One simulated reaction changes one historical exemplar item.\\[0.15cm]
B & A plus display-only explanations.\\[0.15cm]
C & Free text compiles into a category control.\\[0.15cm]
D & An editable preference sentence compiles into a category control.\\[0.15cm]
E & Text, profile and explanations, with duplicate commands removed.\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\vspace{0.25cm}
One boost and one mute request per user, under seeds 42, 43 and 44. The item-feedback proxy is an explicit design choice.
\src{A is not a reconstruction of a commercial feedback algorithm. B must rank identically to A.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Parser results on synthetic language}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Language material & Llama correct & Meaning\\\midrule
Templated inputs & 120/120 & Canonical task\\
Broader synthetic set & 79/100 & Below 85\% target\\
Adversarial subset & 2/10 & Major weakness\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\vspace{0.3cm}
\begin{itemize}
\item 79\% exceeds the 70\% floor. It misses the 85\% target.
\item The rule parser scores 81/100 on the same broader set.
\item The 120 canonical wording patterns informed development.
\end{itemize}
\src{The adversarial subset belongs to the 100 cases. These finite synthetic sets do not establish general language reliability.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Experiment 1: category enforcement}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Top-10 exposure & A & C, D and E\\\midrule
Boost target share, TCP & 11.6\% & 79.2\%\\[0.15cm]
Muted target share, TCER & 6.1\% & 0.0\%\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\vspace{0.4cm}
\begin{itemize}
\item Hard exclusion guarantees zero exposure when parsed correctly.
\item Category-wide boosts affect more items than A's single-item adjustment.
\item Rule and LLM paths produce identical feeds for these canonical Experiment 1 requests.
\end{itemize}
\src{Averages over 865 users and three seeds. This is enforcement evidence, not an LLM advantage.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Steering has a predictive quality cost}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Boost requests & A & C\\\midrule
NDCG@10 & 0.03012 & 0.01637\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\vspace{0.35cm}
\textbf{45.7\% relative decrease.}
\vspace{0.3cm}

Paired mean change: $-0.01375$\par
95\% user-bootstrap interval: $[-0.02086,-0.00655]$.
\vspace{0.3cm}

This post-hoc secondary contrast measures agreement with a historical next item. It does not measure user dissatisfaction after steering.
\src{Post-hoc exploratory interval, outside the frozen primary BH family.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A weak backbone limits the quality claim}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
Uncontrolled ranking & HR@1\\\midrule
SASRec, seed 42 & 0.000\%\\
SASRec, seed 43 & 0.347\%\\
SASRec, seed 44 & 0.462\%\\
Recency popularity & 7.977\%\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\vspace{0.3cm}
Recency pools all users' request history. The model's weights use the older training window. This comparison is descriptive.
\vspace{0.2cm}
The absolute 10 percentage point non-inferiority margin is uninformative at this baseline.
\src{Post-hoc uncontrolled baselines. A pooled HR@1 interval includes zero. No accuracy gain is established.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Experiment 1 conclusion}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Deterministic category control works: TCP 11.6\% to 79.2\%, TCER 6.1\% to zero.
\item Llama parsed 120/120 templated inputs, 79/100 broader synthetic inputs and 2/10 adversarial cases.
\item All 10/10 explanation fact selections equal the template.
\item Boost NDCG@10 falls 45.7\%. Non-inferiority is uninformative.
\item Backbone HR@1 is 0.0--0.46\%, below recency popularity's 8.0\%.
\end{enumerate}
\src{The study establishes bounded enforcement and its limitations. It does not establish an LLM advantage or useful quality preservation.}
\end{frame}

\section{Experiment 2}
\begin{frame}{What the fresh experiment tests}
\small
\begin{itemize}
\item Control success versus rules and item feedback.
\item Requested exposure movement versus item feedback.
\item Historical next-item accuracy as a separate outcome.
\item Earlier practice results were used during development and are separate from the fresh test.
\item Synthetic requests do not measure human experience.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\tiny These claims concern this fixed system and model-generated references.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Four classes of intent}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{2.0cm}P{4.0cm}P{3.7cm}@{}}
\toprule
Class & Illustrative request & Top-10 requirement\\\midrule
Compound & More X and less Y & $n(X)\geq2$, $n(Y)\leq1$\\[0.18cm]
Graded & A bit more X & $2\leq n(X)\leq4$\\
 & Much less X / none of X & $n(X)\leq1$ / $n(X)=0$\\[0.18cm]
Floor & Less X, but not zero & $1\leq n(X)\leq2$\\[0.18cm]
Conditional & More X only if also Y & $n(X\cap Y)\geq2$ and no increase in $X\setminus Y$ count\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\vspace{0.25cm}
Success also requires a full list and positive intended movement. Bands are operational conventions.
\src{X and Y denote opaque categories. Conditional means the same item carries both labels. These are examples, not test requests.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A matched interaction budget}
\begin{itemize}
\item A gets one positive or negative reaction on one historical exemplar.
\item Rule and LLM get the complete utterance once and share the same ranker.
\item Assignments use only earlier history and a committed SHA-256 scheme.
\item Channels share permitted pre-test information. Recency and SASRec use that information differently.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.3cm}
The budget matches interaction count. It does not equalize semantic content, user effort or every possible feedback algorithm.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Prospective design}
\small
The evaluation procedure was fixed before the 100 new requests were created.

100 balanced requests across four classes.\\
Two separate model annotation passes.\\
Reference labels saved before comparison.

Agreement measures model consistency, not human validity.
\vfill
\tiny Both passes used the same reported model family.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Fresh requests and reference labels}
\small
100 selected requests, 25 in each of four classes.\\
31 target category IDs covered by the fixed plan.

Screened against 100 exposed and 48 development texts.\\
No duplicate or frozen close-paraphrase flags selected.

Two passes with the same model family agreed\\
on all 100 initial commands.\\
No human adjudication was performed.
\vfill
\tiny Lexical screening does not prove semantic or pretraining novelty. Model agreement is not human ground truth.
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Earlier development results}
\small
Validated model-reference agreement: Llama 73/100; rules 18/100.\\
All 24 Llama validation errors remain counted.

Induced success: Llama 23.81\%; rules 8.40\%;\\
item feedback 3.92\%.\\
NDCG@10: Llama 0.02461; item feedback 0.03128.

41,508 ranking records were checked.
\vfill
\tiny These earlier questions had been seen during development and do not supply an unseen test.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Prespecified analysis}
\small
User is the analysis unit. Average three seeds within\\
each request/class, then equal eligible classes per user.

5,000 paired user bootstrap samples, seed 2026.\\
Pratt one-sided Wilcoxon with joint BH over 11 tests.

Keep failures, infeasibility and exclusions visible.\\
Report control, HR@1 and NDCG@10 together.
\vfill
\tiny Analysis choices were fixed before reviewing results and were not tuned to them.
\end{frame}

\section{Results and limitations}
\begin{frame}{Fresh model-reference evaluation results}
\small
Validated parser agreement: Llama 76/100; rules 11/100.\\
Llama operational/validation failures: 23, retained.

Induced success: Llama 20.145\%; rules 3.854\%;\\
item feedback 3.815\%. Conditional requests: 0 successes.\\
NDCG@10: Llama 0.02580; item feedback 0.03184.

41,520 ranking rows; 2,595 full score vectors.
\vfill
\tiny These measured control and quality outcomes concern model-authored synthetic requests, not independent human validation.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Contribution and limits}
\small
Under model-generated reference labels, language control\\
exceeded the rule parser and single-item feedback on\\
the prespecified control measures.

Translation, enforcement and quality remain observable.\\
Failures remained in every reported comparison.

Human experience, naturalistic language and other\\
LLM models require separate evidence.
\vfill
\tiny The weak backbone, opaque categories and historical next-item proxy limit recommendation-quality interpretation.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Interpretation of the results}
\small
\begin{itemize}
\item Language translated richer multi-category requests.
\item Deterministic rules applied validated controls.
\item Pooled control gains came with lower next-item NDCG@10.
\item Conditional requests had no successful controls.
\item Synthetic wording and model references limit generalization.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\tiny Category control and historical next-item prediction are separate outcomes.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What these results can establish}
\small
\begin{itemize}
\item The 100 synthetic requests were evaluated across 865 users.
\item Reference commands came from two passes using the same model family.
\item Human understanding and satisfaction were not measured.
\item Full E1 replay on Windows and Colab remains unverified.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\tiny The separate research archive retains the full study records.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: metric definitions}
\begin{description}
\item[TCP@10] Target-category items divided by ten for boost requests.
\item[TCER@10] Target-category items divided by ten for mute requests.
\item[HR@1] One if the first item equals the held-out target, otherwise zero.
\item[NDCG@10] For one relevant item at rank $r\leq10$, $1/\log_2(r+1)$. Zero if absent.
\end{description}
\vspace{0.3cm}
Both exposure metrics have the same arithmetic and opposite desired directions. Historical quality does not directly measure preference satisfaction after intervention.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: why non-inferiority is uninformative}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{6.6cm}r@{}}
\toprule
Pooled Experiment 1 quantity & Value\\\midrule
A HR@1 baseline & 0.2505\%\\
C minus A, mean & +0.193 pp\\

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Backup: intended-direction movement}
\small
\[
D(S)=\frac{\sum_{r=1}^{10}\mathbf{1}[i_r\in S]/\log_2(r+1)}{\sum_{r=1}^{10}1/\log_2(r+1)}
\]
$\Delta D$ is the channel value minus its uncontrolled value.
\begin{tabular}{@{}P{2.7cm}P{7.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
Class & Signed movement $M$\\\midrule
Compound & $\min(\Delta D(X),-\Delta D(Y))$\\
Boost / reduction & $\Delta D(X)$ / $-\Delta D(X)$\\
Floor & $-\Delta D(X)$, zero if the floor is violated\\
Conditional & $\Delta D(X\cap Y)-\max(0,\Delta D(X\setminus Y))$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\vspace{0.25cm}
Induced satisfaction requires a full list, the class band and $M>10^{-12}$. This operational measure is not Shannon information.
\end{frame}
